Find Your Profitable Idea

Stop overthinking. Learn how to validate business ideas in 24 hours using the Problem-Solution Framework.

The Biggest Lie About Business Ideas

Everyone tells you: "Find your passion!" "Follow your dreams!" "Do what you love!"

That's terrible advice.

The Truth:

Great businesses aren't built on passion. They're built on solving painful problems for people willing to pay for solutions.

The 3-Question Validation Test

Before you build anything, answer these 3 questions:

1. What problem are you solving?

Not "I help people be more productive." That's vague.

Try: "I help freelance designers who spend 10+ hours/week on invoicing and admin work automate it down to 30 minutes."

Specific problem + specific person + specific outcome = good business idea.

2. Is the problem painful enough?

People don't pay for "nice to have." They pay for "need to have."

Ask: If you DON'T solve this problem, what happens?

  • "They lose money" → Good pain
  • "They waste time" → Good pain
  • "They feel stressed/overwhelmed" → Good pain
  • "It's mildly annoying" → Bad pain (won't pay)

3. Can you reach these people?

Best idea in the world means nothing if you can't find customers.

Ask: Where do these people hang out?

  • Specific Facebook/Reddit groups?
  • LinkedIn (what job titles?)
  • Instagram hashtags?
  • Industry forums/communities?

If you can't answer this, your niche is too broad.

The Problem-First Framework

Most people start with solutions: "I'll build an app!" "I'll start a coaching business!"

That's backward. Start with problems.

Exercise: Find 10 Problems (30 minutes)

Go to these places and write down problems people complain about:

  1. Reddit: r/Entrepreneur, r/smallbusiness, niche subreddits
  2. Facebook Groups: Join 3-5 groups in your niche
  3. Amazon Reviews: Read 1-star reviews of products in your niche
  4. YouTube Comments: Find videos about your topic, read comments
  5. Twitter: Search "[your niche] problem" or "[niche] frustrating"

Look for patterns. What problems come up over and over?

Real Example:

"I spent 30 minutes reading r/freelance. Top complaints: clients not paying on time, not knowing how to price, spending too much time on admin. Boom—3 potential business ideas."

How to Pick THE Idea

You now have 10 problems. How do you pick one?

Rate each problem 1-10 on these criteria:

  • Pain (1-10): How painful is this problem?
  • Frequency (1-10): How often does this problem happen?
  • Willingness to Pay (1-10): Will people pay to solve it?
  • Your Interest (1-10): Can you stay interested in solving this for 12 months?

Pick the problem with the highest total score (max 40 points).

Validate Before You Build

Don't build a product yet. Test if people will actually pay.

The 48-Hour Validation Challenge:

  1. Post your idea in 3-5 relevant communities
  2. Ask: "Would you pay $X for a solution to [problem]?"
  3. If 10+ people say "yes" → you've got something
  4. If everyone says "no" or "maybe" → pick a different problem

Real-World Examples

Example 1: The Email Templates Business

Problem: Freelancers waste 5+ hours/week writing client emails

Solution: Sell a pack of 50 email templates for $47

Result: $10K in first month from Reddit + Twitter

Example 2: The Notion Templates Shop

Problem: Creators struggle to organize content calendars

Solution: Pre-built Notion templates for content planning

Result: $50K/year passive income on Gumroad

Example 3: The Launch Checklist SaaS

Problem: Entrepreneurs forget critical launch steps

Solution: Interactive checklist tool ($19/month)

Result: 200 paying customers in 90 days
