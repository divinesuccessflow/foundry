Define Your Customer Avatar

Get crystal clear on WHO you're serving. If you're targeting everyone, you're reaching no one.

The Rule:

The riches are in the niches.

Why "Everyone" is the Wrong Answer

When I ask "Who's your customer?" and you say "anyone who needs [thing]," you've already lost.

Here's why:

  • You can't write copy that speaks to everyone
  • You don't know where to find them
  • You can't create a product that solves everyone's problem
  • Your marketing budget gets wasted on the wrong people

Bad: "My product is for entrepreneurs."

Good: "My product is for solo consultants who bill hourly and want to switch to value-based pricing but don't know how."

The Customer Avatar Framework

Give your ideal customer a name and a face. Make them REAL.

Meet Sarah (Example Avatar)

Name: Sarah the Stuck Consultant

Age: 32

Job: Marketing Manager at a tech company

Income: $75K/year

Location: Austin, TX

Goal: Start a consulting business on the side, eventually go full-time

Pain Points:

  • Overwhelmed by conflicting advice from gurus
  • Doesn't know how to price her services
  • Afraid of quitting her job without a safety net
  • Stuck in analysis paralysis—bought 5 courses, finished none

What She Says: "I know I should start, but I don't know what to do first. Everyone says something different."

The 4 Layers of Customer Understanding

Layer 1: Demographics (The Facts)

Basic info about who they are:

  • Age, gender, location
  • Job title, industry, income
  • Education level, marital status

Layer 2: Psychographics (The Why)

What they believe and value:

  • Core values (freedom, security, status?)
  • Beliefs about success, money, work
  • Personality traits (risk-taker? perfectionist?)

Layer 3: Behavior (How They Act)

Where they spend time and money:

  • Social platforms (Instagram? LinkedIn? Reddit?)
  • Content consumption (blogs, YouTube, podcasts?)
  • Buying habits (impulsive? research-heavy?)

Layer 4: Language (What They Say)

The exact words they use:

  • "I'm stuck in analysis paralysis"
  • "I don't know where to start"
  • "I keep buying courses but never finish them"

Use THEIR words in your marketing. It's like magic.

How to Research Your Avatar

5 Places to Find Real Customer Language:

  1. Amazon Reviews: Read 1-star and 5-star reviews of competitors' products. What do people love? What pisses them off?
  2. Reddit/Forums: Search "[your niche] frustrated" or "[niche] struggling"
  3. YouTube Comments: Find popular videos in your niche, read ALL the comments
  4. Facebook Groups: Join 3-5 groups, observe what questions come up repeatedly
  5. Interviews: Talk to 5-10 people who match your avatar (best method!)

The Interview Script

If you can talk to real people (even 5), you'll learn more than months of guessing.

10 Questions to Ask:

  1. What's your biggest challenge with [topic] right now?
  2. How long have you been dealing with this problem?
  3. What have you tried so far to solve it?
  4. Why didn't those solutions work?
  5. If you could wave a magic wand and fix this, what would change?
  6. How much time/money is this problem costing you?
  7. What would it be worth to you to solve this?
  8. Where do you usually go for advice on [topic]?
  9. Who do you trust in this space?
  10. If I built [your solution], would you be interested?

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Mistake #1: Making up an avatar

Don't just imagine who your customer is. Go FIND them and TALK to them.

❌ Mistake #2: Too broad

"Entrepreneurs aged 25-55" is not specific enough. Narrow it down.

❌ Mistake #3: Targeting yourself

Your avatar doesn't have to be you. Target who you can HELP, not who you are.

Your Day 2 Action Steps

  • ☐ Fill out demographics, psychographics, behavior, and language
  • ☐ Research 3 of the 5 sources listed above (30-60 min)
  • ☐ Bonus: Interview 2-3 real people who match your avatar
